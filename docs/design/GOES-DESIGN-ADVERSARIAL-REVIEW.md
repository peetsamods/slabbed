## 2. Attacking the One Rule — one real hole, two spec gaps

**THE HOLE (highest-priority finding): SIDE inheritance at depth breaks the honest raycast's own contract.** §2 row 2 promises chained side slabs inherit stored magnitude at any depth, cantilevered over air. But the deep-owner probe terminates at the first air cell walking up from the marched cell (`SlabbedOffsetRaycast.java:182-183` `state.isAir() → return false`), and the in-code INVARIANT comment says outright: *"real placement always accumulates depth through a contiguous support column, so it cannot produce that shape."* A side-inherited slab at ≤ −2.0 over air has ≥1 air cell between its visible body and its grid cell → the probe breaks → **the body is untargetable**. The §1.2 premise ("the aim arrives server-side in the use packet") silently fails for the deepest outputs of the design's own rule: you can place it but never aim at it again. R6 covers only the *server* seam. TEST 17's towers were contiguous, so this was never live-exercised. Fix options: store-aware air-gap tolerance in the probe, or cap SIDE depth to the raycast contract — either way it needs an `OffsetRaycastDeepLaneTest` row for a cantilevered −2.0/−2.5 side slab.

**Spec gaps (lesser):** (a) UP-click on a lowered **TOP**-slab owner — §1.3.4 covers only BOTTOM+UP→DOUBLE; rule-1-vs-rule-4 precedence for TOP+UP is implicit (the C2 RED test `slabOnMinus1TopSlabOwnerLandsFlush` pins the answer; the normative text should state it, since this is exactly the 6-row dy-split geometry). (b) The B1 closure (visible underside of −1.0 owner → `below(2)` at dy 0.0) — arithmetic verified correct, but for attachment-requiring families (hanging lantern/chain) the grid cell above `below(2)` is air, so vanilla `canSurvive` refuses; sound only for free-standing families, should be scoped. (c) Occlusion-refusal guards only aimed routes — pistons/dispensers/frozen-OFF worlds can still mint overlapping visible bodies; the resolver's behavior when aiming at an overlap (first-hit-wins) should be stated, not left to the raycast's accident. The DOWN-face NaN-support case is fine (stored-first with live fallback is total).

## 3. Capture point — VERIFIED against real 26.2 bytecode

Decompiled from the project's own loom-mapped jar: `BlockItem.place` calls `placeBlock` (bc 63) then `Block.setPlacedBy` (bc 171) before RETURN. `DoorBlock.setPlacedBy` places the UPPER half itself and never calls super (bytecode confirmed — no `invokespecial Block.setPlacedBy`). `BedBlock.setPlacedBy` calls super FIRST then places HEAD via `setBlockAndUpdate` — confirming both the half-formed-FOOT capture bug and that **HEAD exists by place-RETURN**. `DoubleHighBlockItem.placeBlock` only pre-fills air/water above then supers; the real UPPER comes from setPlacedBy — inside place. `SolidBucketItem.useOn` invokespecials `BlockItem.useOn` → place, so powder snow's cells exist at RETURN. The single-capture-point claim **holds** for every family I could check. One nuance the doc under-states: in C2, slab placements are double-captured (stale setPlacedBy write, resolver RETURN overwrite) since §3 removes the old call only with C3 per R1 — benign by ordering, but say so and pin it.

## 6. Perf

Placement-time resolver cost: fine. The trap is C5: moving thin layers/powder snow to the logical triad puts `getYOffset` on `getShape`/`getCollisionShape` for the most numerous natural blocks in snowy biomes, and natural fill is **permanently NaN-stored** (AIMLESS uncaptured) — so every collision/outline query on every snow layer pays a full `getYOffsetInner` lane walk (multiple chunk-attachment lookups + ~10 beta35 lane calls) forever. This is precisely the shipped-lag-twice class the PERF hygiene law exists for; R7/R8 discuss correctness, not this cost. Amendment: AIM-KEYED family + NaN store ⇒ constant flush **fast-path before the lane walk**, and extend the allocation-regression gametest to collision queries on snow layers. (Bonus fact supporting C5: `CarpetDyShapeMixin` client-only today means carpet outline/collision already desync across sides — moving it to common is a fix, not just a refactor.)

## 8. Strongest counter-design, steelmanned

*Fix the lanes in place with depth-parametric reads* (the TEST 15/17 depth-cap-removal pattern continued): smallest diffs, no new authority, no capture relocation risk. It loses on the merits: (1) live-lane widening retroactively moves already-placed blocks — a direct LAW §3 violation and the cross-port anchor-widening law (R4/R5); (2) the lanes are **aim-blind by construction** (the hit is discarded before any height decision — verified at `:1467-1497`), and TEST 17's boundary failures (pot/bed at exactly −1.0, TOP-vs-flush disambiguation) are aim-dependent, hence unfixable by any read that never sees the aim; (3) the alternative capture point (setPlacedBy + patch each overrider) has to individually chase DoorBlock/BedBlock/DiodeBlock/... and *still* reorder marker authoring — it converges on place-RETURN with more moving parts. The proposed design wins on law-fidelity and total risk; its weakest unexamined dependency is the aim-attribution contract at depth (finding #1 above).
